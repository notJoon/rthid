# Pan profiling results, 2026-11-26

Measurements behind the terminal output changes on the `perf/terminal-output`
branch, taken with `scripts/profile/run.py`. Use them as a baseline for later
work and rerun the harness rather than trusting the absolute numbers on
another machine.

## Setup

- Apple Terminal on macOS, window about 200x54 cells (the harness asks for 60
  rows, the screen fit 54 or 55).
- Image `tests/fixtures/IMG_7139.PNG`, shrunk by the app to 1024x653.
- A pty proxy injects left button drags at 120 events per second for 6 seconds,
  which caps the frame rate at 120. A few pairs ran at 240 events per second.
- Each comparison builds both commits in separate worktrees and runs them
  interleaved (before, after, three times each) because machine load moved the
  absolute numbers by up to 10 percent between runs.
- Main thread shares come from `sample` during the drag. "write" is time blocked
  in write(2), "encode" is crossterm command formatting and the backend draw
  loop, "render" is building cells.
- Zoom names follow `Zoom` in `src/viewport.rs`: In(1) is 100 percent, In(2) is
  200 percent.

## Finding

Panning a zoomed photo is bound by how fast the terminal takes in output, about
12 to 15 MB/s in Apple Terminal, not by rendering. At the start, 77 percent of
the main thread waited in write(2) while building cells took under 1 percent.
Only fewer bytes per frame raised the frame rate.

## Steps

Frame rate while dragging at In(1), mean of three runs. Numbers from different
steps were measured on different days of load, so compare within a row pair.

| Commit | Change | Original fps | Dithered fps | Bytes per frame |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| baseline | stock backend, stdout line buffer | 38 to 41 | 42 to 53 | Original 301 KB, Dithered 213 KB |
| buffered frames | 1 MB `BufWriter`, synchronized update | 41.2 to 41.8 (no change) | 53.2 to 48.7 (noise) | unchanged, writes per frame 298 to 2 |
| 256 colors while dragging | Original cells use xterm 256 colors during a drag | 37.9 to 62.0 | unchanged | Original 301 to 137 KB |
| background spaces | cells with equal halves drawn as a space | 64.4 to 72.5 | 58.1 to 69.0 | Original 134 to 122 KB, Dithered 209 to 170 KB |
| eliding backend | send only the color that changed, skip fg of spaces | 68.6 to 76.0 | 64.8 to 81.5 | Original 122 to 91 KB, Dithered 170 to 118 KB |

At In(2) every step after the 256 color change already reached the 120 fps
input cap. Later steps showed there as less data and write time: background
spaces cut bytes per frame by 34 to 40 percent at In(2) and In(4), and the
eliding backend by another 12 to 15 percent. At 240 events per second In(2)
kept up in both modes after the background space change.

Buffered frames did not raise the frame rate. They cut write calls from about
one per KiB to two per frame and encode time from about 9 to 3 percent of the
main thread, and the saved time moved into waiting on the terminal.

## Offline byte counts

A counting encoder that matched the stock crossterm backend byte for byte over
726 frames estimated the encoder changes before they were built (200x55 view,
one dot pan per frame):

| Source | Zoom | Stock | Eliding | With background spaces too |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Original 24 bit | In(1) | 393 KB | 0 percent | 0 percent |
| Original 256 colors | In(1) | 181 KB | 24 percent less | 31 percent less |
| Dithered black and white | In(1) | 289 KB | 28 percent less | 42 percent less |
| Original 24 bit | In(2) | 212 KB | 0 percent | 50 percent less |
| Dithered black and white | In(2) | 112 KB | 0 percent | 44 percent less |

Measured savings were a little smaller than the estimates because the real
screen also holds the minimap and the side panel.

## Not tried

- Terminal scroll regions for pans, which would make bytes scale with the pan
  distance instead of the view size.
- Quantizing wheel and keyboard pans.
- Other terminals. GPU terminals take in output roughly ten times faster, so the
  same changes matter less there.
